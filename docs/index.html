Built around how people here eat

Star ratings won't tell you where the cheese dip is best or which dairy bar still has a line at the window. Arkansas Eats is organized the way people here eat: cheese dip, barbecue, catfish houses, dairy bars and drive-ins, homemade pie and the Food Hall of Fame, each place checked in fall 2026 against a 2025–26 source. Free, with no catch

The app costs nothing, shows no ads and sells nothing inside, and there's no account to make. The lists ship inside the app, so they open right away and keep working when the signal drops out on a back road.

What stays on your phone

Arkansas Eats collects nothing about you: no sign-in, no analytics, no ads. Location, if you turn it on, only puts the nearest places first, and your saved places are kept on the phone or iPad. The website has no cookies and no trackers. The privacy policy has the details.

Questions

Is Arkansas Eats free?

Completely: no price, no ads, no in-app purchases and no account to create. It's an iPhone and iPad app on its way to the App Store, with no Android version yet; until it's out, the web search here works in any browser.

Where do the lists come from?

Two kinds of source. The cheese dip, barbecue, catfish, dairy bar, pie, Food Hall of Fame and oldest-places lists are our own research from fall 2026, with a 2025 or 2026 source behind each place: Arkansas Tourism's own pages, the Arkansas Food Hall of Fame or a dated local story. The wider directory of 7,279 restaurants starts from open map listings (Overture Maps), kept by the rule that proved reliable in states that publish official lists.

Where's the cheese dip?

Our cheese dip list has 15 hand-checked stops on Arkansas Tourism's Cheese Dip Trail, from the Heights and Riverdale in Little Rock to Conway, Hot Springs, Eureka Springs and Fayetteville.

Does it show restaurant inspections?

Yes, as the Arkansas Department of Health lists them. Where ADH's search lists a place, the app shows its latest inspection's date and the number of observations on ADH's report, with ADH's note that an inspection is a snapshot in time. ADH gives no score or grade, and the app computes none; no list is ranked by inspections.

Does it show which places serve alcohol?

Where a place matches an on-premises permit on DFA Alcoholic Beverage Control's monthly list (restaurant mixed drink, beer or wine on premises, or a private club), the app says it's licensed to serve on the premises. Otherwise it shows unknown, never no: many counties are dry, and restaurants there may serve under a private club permit.

Does it show ratings and reviews?

Not from us. Tapping a place opens Apple Maps' own card inside the app, with Apple's current ratings, hours, photos and directions. None of our lists is sorted by anyone's rating.

Does it need my location?

No. Location only matters for putting the nearest places first. If you share it, it stays on your iPhone or iPad and never reaches us, and the app works fine without it. On this website, Near me reads your location inside your browser and nowhere else.

A place has closed, or something's wrong. How do I tell you?

Write to nick@eatsranked.com with the place's name, its town and what changed. Corrections ship with the next data update.
